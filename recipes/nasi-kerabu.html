Nasi Kerabu

Home

Description

Nasi Kerabu is a staple dish originating from the East Coast of Malaysia, mainly from the state of Kelantan. This dish is packed full components and ingredients that makes it a very lively dish. The most striking feature of this dish is the blue colored rice as a result of using Bunga Telang (Butterfly Pea Flower) as colouring. Accompanying the rice are stir-fried spicy gravy, fish floss, stuffed peppers, sambal, budu, and deep-fried battered fish to round off this complete package of a dish.

Ingredients

For the blue rice:

  • 3 cups of rice
  • 4.5 to 5 cups of Bunga Telang water
  • Plentiful of Bunga Telang
  • Plentiful of hot water
  • 1 teaspoon of salt
  • 1 to 2 piece of lemongrass
  • 3 kaffir lime leaves

For the stir-fry gravy:

  • 6 stalks of dried chilies
  • 6 pieces of shallots
  • 4 cloves of garlic
  • 1 inch of galangal
  • 1 piece of lemongrass
  • 1 piece of lemongrass, bruised
  • 3 kaffir lime leaves
  • 1 bowl of coconut milk
  • 2 spoon of palm sugar
  • 1 piece of tamarind slice
  • 1/2 ladle of budu
  • salt
  • seasoning powder

For the fish floss:

  • 400g of scad fish
  • 2 piece of tamarind slice (asam keping)
  • water
  • grated coconut
  • 3 piece of lemongrass
  • 1 inch galangal
  • 1 inch ginger
  • 1 sweet onion/yellow onion
  • 1/2 spoon of black pepper balls
  • salt
  • seasoning powder
  • 1 piece of palm sugar

For the stuffed pepper (solok lada)

  • 200g of scad fish
  • 5 pieces of shallots
  • 4 cloves of garlic
  • 1 inch garlic
  • 1 teaspoon ofblack pepper balls
  • grated coconut
  • 1 teaspoon of salt
  • 1/2 spoon of sugar
  • seasoning powder
  • 8 piece of green chili peppers
  • 200ml of coconut milk

For the sambal:

  • 6 to 8 stalks of red bird's eye chilies
  • 2 to 3 stalks of large red chilies
  • 1/2 cup of vinegar
  • 2 spoons of sugar
  • 1 teaspoon salt

For the Budu Masak:

  • 1/2 cup of budu
  • 1 piece of palm sugar
  • 1 piece of lemongrass, bruised
  • 1/2 inch of ginger
  • 1/2 inch of galangal
  • 1/2 cup of water

For the deep-fried battered fish:

  • 6 to 8 piece of indian mackerel
  • 1 cup of rice flour
  • 2 spoon of flour
  • 1 egg
  • 1 teaspoon of vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon of slaked lime water
  • 1 teaspoon of salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon of turmeric powder
  • water
  • oil

For the traditional salad (ulam-ulaman):

  • beansprouts
  • long beans
  • water celery
  • Vietnamese coriander
  • torch ginger flower
  • cucumber
  • cabbage
  • fried fish crackers
  • hard-boiled egg

Steps

For the blue rice:

  1. Soak the Bunga Telang with hot water until the water turns deep blue and then filter the water.
  2. Wash the rice and put it in a rice cooker.
  3. Put in the lemongrass, kaffir lime and salt. Stir the mix until the aromatic smell rises.
  4. Pour 4.5 to 5 cups of Bunga Telang water and cook the rice as usual until perfectly cooked.

For the stir-fry gravy:

  1. Blend dried chilies, shallots, garlic, ginger, galangal, and lemongrass in a blender until smooth.
  2. Heat up the cooking oil and stir-fry the blended mix until the oil separates, fragrant smelling and slightly crisp and .
  3. Put in the palm sugar, crushed lemongrass and kaffir lime leaves.
  4. Pour the coconut milk and stir slowly so that it is properly mixed in.
  5. Put in the tamarind slice, budu, salt and seasoning powder.
  6. Cook until the gravy is slightly thick and the taste is fatty, salty, sweet dan smoothly sour.

For the fish floss:

  1. Boil the fish with the tamarind slice with the fish half submersed in water until fully cooked.
  2. After boiling the fish, separate the meat and the bones of the fish.
  3. Blend lemongrass, galangal, ginger, onion, and black pepper with a little water until smooth.
  4. Fry the grated coconut without oil until slightly dry and brownish, then put aside.
  5. Put in the blended mix into a pan without oil and stir until dry and fragrant.
  6. Put in the fish meat, palm sugar, salt and seasoning powder.
  7. Mix with fried grated coconut and cook until the floss crumbles and well combined.
  8. Stop cooking until the floss is still slightly damp so that the texture is not too dry.

For the stuffed pepper:

  1. Get rid of the bones in the fish and keep the fish fillet.
  2. Blend the raw fish fillet with shallots, garlic, ginger, and black pepper until the paste becomes thick and smooth.
  3. Mix the fish paste with grated coconut, salt, sugar, and seasoning powder until well mixed.
  4. Rest the mix in the fridge until it becomes firm and easily used for stuffing.
  5. Cut the pepper vertically but not until it splits so that theres an opening to be stuffed.
  6. Stuff the mix in the pepper neatly.
  7. Arrange in a tray, sprinkle a little bit of salt and pour some coconut milk on top. Do not mix the coconut milk in the stuffing.
  8. Steam for 15 to 20 minute with medium heat until fully cooked and smooth.

For the sambal:

  1. Cut up the red bird's eye chilies and large red chilies.
  2. Put the chilies, vinegar, salt, and sugar in a blender and blend without water.
  3. Makes sure the sambal tastes balanced between spicy, sour, and sweet.

For the budu:

  1. Mix budu, palm suger, galangal, ginger and water in a pot.
  2. Cook over a small fire and mix until the palm sugar dissolves
  3. Continue cooking until it is slightly thick and fragrant.

For the deep-fried battered fish:

  1. Clean the Indian Mackerel, toss till dry and mix some salt and turmeric.
  2. In a bowl, mix in rice flour, flour, egg, vinegar, slaked lime water, salt and a little bit of turmeric.
  3. Pour cold water gradually until the mix is medium thick.
  4. Let it rest for 10 to 15 minutes.
  5. Heat up cooking oil until it's ready for frying.
  6. Dip the fish in the batter and fry until crispy. Sprinkle a bit of the batter into the frying pan for some crunchy flour go along with the fish
  7. Be sure to not fry too many fish at the same time to maintain oil temperature and crispness.
  8. Stop frying until the fish is yellowish and drain the excess oil away.

Serving guide

  1. Cut all the ingredients for the traditional salad thinly.
  2. Put the blue rice on a plate.
  3. Arrange the floss, stuffed pepper, battered fried fish, hard-boiled egg, and traditional salad around the rice.
  4. Serve with the gravy, cooked budu, sambal, and fried fish crackers.
  5. Mix every component of the dish well for the most complete taste.
